The problem you already feel

Finance rarely fails because an expert is bad. It fails in the gaps between them.

Internal auditors, external auditors, the CS, the tax consultant, the banker, the lender, the investor, your own finance team. Each owns a slice. Each optimizes for their own scope and their own liability. No one owns whether the thing actually closes.

So it falls to you. The one person least able to afford the distraction — and the most expensive to waste.

What you see

Tasks slip through the cracks between consultants. Things arrive late, missing, or done twice.

What's actually happening

No single party holds end-to-end accountability. Every handoff quietly loses information and ownership.

What it costs you

You become the integrator — a single point of failure at the worst possible moment. A raise that stalls. An audit that blows up. Value destroyed in diligence.

Cracks are cheap to fix. A failed raise is not. We exist to prevent the catastrophic by relentlessly fixing the cheap.

The promised land

One party in the room, accountable for the finish line.

Imagine the next raise where you hear from one team — not twelve. Where the numbers reconcile to a single, current source of truth that every advisor points to. Where the risk that would have surfaced as a 2 a.m. crisis reaches you as a calm, early flag with a recommendation already attached.

You stop being the glue. You go back to running the company.

What we are — and are not

We orchestrate the regulated work. We never perform it.

That single line is our whole advantage. An auditor can't be neutral about their own audit. A CFO can't oversee the CFO function. A banker is paid only if the deal closes their way. Only an outside party with no regulated stake can hold the whole system accountable. That seat is empty. We take it.
